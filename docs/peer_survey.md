## What this means for us
1. **Both leading entries put physics into a *smoother*, not a forward recursion**: AxisTilted2 solves one Gaussian smoothing problem with accelerometer-bias and gravity states over a 64-s context, and supervises the integrated trajectory directly with a segment ATE loss. Their drone ATE 1.00 (ours 1.20), tail AVE 3.2 (ours 3.8–4.1). These are exactly the two things our recursion line lacked: (a) bias / gravity in the state (our increments absorb the bias), (b) a loss on the *integrated path* rather than per-window velocity.
2. **Context length**: 64 s (AxisTilted2), 32 s + 16 s overlap (xunden, others) vs our 40 s.
3. **Family-grouped folds**: they bind 14 correlated recording pairs into three folds — consistent with our "three rulers are three populations" conclusion.
4. Our car/dog are better than the top entry; the difference is drone (tail + group D) and human ATE.
5. Report style to borrow from AxisTilted2: an honest decision ledger (what did not work and why it stopped), Tables III–V pasted straight from the official output.

## Addendum: BShankar1080's report (the README is one line; the details are in report.pdf)
- 5.2 M: per-window 1-D ResNet (200 → 25, mean+max pooling, 256-d) + window mean/std → 2-layer biGRU(256) over 32-window chunks (inference: 64 windows overlapping 16, centre taken) → MosaicIMU-style 4 zero-initialised residual expert MLPs (chunk-mean features softmaxed against 4 learned prototypes) → drone gate (label BCE 0.1) scaling a residual MLP over drag-physics features [ā/g, √max(|f|²−g², 0), |ω|] → head = MLP + context-gated linear term v += M(h)(ā/g − c(h)) (rotor drag law).
- Loss: per-window Euclidean (AVE) only; multi-horizon drift losses neutral to slightly worse. **The only useful augmentation: physically correct time warp s ~ U(0.7, 2.0)** (= our time dilation + S-fast), full test 0.295 → 0.270. 8000 steps, AdamW, bf16, dropout 0.3.
- Six seeds, official 0.2379–0.2557 (spread 0.018, entirely from the four fastest drone sequences); the best full-test seed was chosen (disclosed). 185 training runs, 25 Kaggle, 23 official.
- Findings that agree with ours: val does not predict public (0.254 / 0.409); **sprint detection is easy, sprint speed is unobservable without that vehicle's drag coefficient**; the two sprints come from public UZH-FPV (and Blackbird) data, which the organisers ruled out; drift losses, longer context, rotation augmentation, fast-flight gate, drag-law head, gyro mid-point frame, post-hoc smoothing and GBDT residuals were all useless.
- What they did right and we did not: short chunks (32 windows; L=128/256 worse), a time-warp ceiling of 2.0 (ours 1.5), "the single most important component = short-range bidirectional context".
